How to Choose a Camping Tent

Capacity ratings assume slim sleepers side by side — for comfort, size up one person. Then check these specs before price:

  • Waterproof rating: 2000mm for summer rain; 3000mm+ for real storms
  • Poles: fiberglass is budget-friendly; aluminum is lighter and lasts longer
  • Two doors & vestibules matter more than floor space for 3–4 person tents

Reselling? The 2-person dome is the volume staple in most markets. See our 2-Person Dome Tent.

Backpack Capacity: 40L, 60L or 80L?

Match volume to trip length, not ambition — an oversized pack just gets filled with weight.

  • 30–40L: day hikes and overnight trips
  • 50–65L: the sweet spot for 3–5 day trips — our 60L covers most buyers
  • 70L+: winter trips or expeditions with bulky insulation

Also check the back system: an adjustable torso and padded hip belt sell better than extra pockets. See our 60L Hiking Backpack.

Sleeping Bag Temperature Ratings, Explained

Under the ISO/EN standard every rated bag carries three numbers — and they are not interchangeable:

  • Comfort rating: an average sleeper stays warm all night — the number that matters
  • Limit rating: a warm sleeper can just manage, curled up
  • Extreme rating: survival only — never sell on this number

Down packs smaller and lighter but loses insulation when wet; synthetic fill is bulkier yet keeps working when damp. Mummy shapes trap heat for cold nights; rectangular bags suit car camping. A 3-season bag is the volume staple — see our 3-Season Sleeping Bag.

How to Choose Trekking Poles
  • Material: aluminium is tougher and cheaper; carbon is lighter but can crack on hard side impact
  • Locking: lever quick-locks clamp tight without tools; twist locks can slip when grit gets in
  • Grip: cork moulds to the hand; foam is light and budget-friendly; rubber suits cold weather
  • Basket & tip: small baskets for summer, wide for snow; carbide tips grip rock and ice

See our Folding Trekking Poles — they fold small for travel to the trailhead.

How to Choose a Folding Camping Chair
  • Weight capacity: check the rated load and leave a margin above your typical user's weight
  • Folded size & carry weight: decides whether it suits car campers and festival-goers
  • Frame & fabric: steel is strong and economical, aluminium cuts weight; look for Oxford weave with reinforced stitching
  • Comfort extras: a footrest makes it a lounger; cup holder and side pocket keep essentials off the ground

See our Folding Camping Chair with Footrest.

How to Choose a Portable Camping Stove
  • Fuel: a screw-on gas canister is the simplest system — no priming or pumping, canisters widely available
  • Heat & boil time: judge boil speed alongside fuel efficiency and simmer control
  • Piezo ignition: push-button lighting without matches (pack a backup lighter anyway)
  • Weight & packed size: folding pot supports pack small for hikers; stability matters more for car camping
  • Stability & wind: wide supports and a sheltered flame keep pans steady and lit

See our Portable Camping Stove.

How to Choose a Rechargeable Headlamp

Lumens measure total output, not beam reach — a focused beam throws usable light further than a flood at the same count, so check beam distance too.

  • Rechargeable beats disposable: USB-C is now the expected standard
  • Runtime: judge hours on medium mode, not the turbo headline
  • Water resistance: IPX4 for rain splashes; IPX6+ for downpours
  • Red-light mode preserves night vision; a tilting head aims the beam without neck strain

See our Rechargeable LED Headlamp.

How to Choose a Camping Lantern
  • Brightness & modes: several levels — down for inside a tent, up for cooking; warm light for evenings, white for tasks
  • Battery & runtime: rechargeable, judged on a medium setting
  • USB & power-bank function: refill from car or solar; top up a phone or headlamp in camp
  • Hanging: a handle or hook spreads light from above; a flat base sits steady on a table
  • Water resistance: splash-resistant minimum for rain and wet ground

See our USB Camping Lantern & Power Bank.

How to Choose a Packable Windbreaker
  • Water-resistant ≠ waterproof: a resistant finish sheds drizzle but soaks through in sustained rain; full waterproofing needs a membrane and sealed seams, at more bulk and cost
  • Breathability: blocks wind chill while letting sweat vapour escape
  • Packability: should pack into its own pocket or pouch
  • Weight: light enough to carry just in case
  • Details: adjustable or pack-away hood, smooth full zip, secure zipped pockets, sealed cuffs and hem

Enough for breezy ridges and light showers; step up to a rain jacket for prolonged heavy rain. See our Packable Windbreaker Jacket.

How to Choose an Insulated Stainless Steel Bottle
  • Vacuum insulation: double walls with air drawn out — hot stays hot, cold stays cold, outer wall stays comfortable
  • Capacity: smaller for desk and school bags, mid-size as the all-rounder, larger for long trips without refills
  • Lid: screw-on seals safest in a bag; flip/straw for one-handed drinking; wide mouth is easiest to fill and clean
  • Fit: slim shapes suit pack side pockets and car cup holders
  • Care: stainless resists dents, holds no flavours, and wipes clean
